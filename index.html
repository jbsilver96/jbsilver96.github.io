01 / Research agenda

Capability is only the beginning.

Our proposed studies examine the distance between what a system can do once and what a person can depend on in practice.

01

What becomes possible?

Find the tasks that individuals and small teams can complete with AI, and compare them with a clearly defined baseline.

Observe: task completion, quality, scope.

02

What stays reliable?

Repeat the work. Track failures, human interventions, and sensitivity to changes in prompts, tools, and conditions.

Observe: repeatability, error, oversight.

03

What becomes worthwhile?

Ask where a workflow is useful after accounting for setup, review, maintenance, and the people affected by its use.

Observe: effort, cost, practical value.

Planned study

From sources to a usable brief

Compare a small AI-assisted research workflow with a manual baseline. Define a task set and scoring rubric, repeat trials, and record factual errors, time spent, and human corrections.

Planned research sequence

Test. Reproduce. Reconsider.

Begin with a small reproducible study and lab notes. Follow with a second study and reusable artifact, seek an external reproduction, then use a third study and external review to inform a synthesis. Scope will follow the evidence.

Project disclosure: Current NYC is a founder-owned project. Evaluating our own work creates a conflict of interest; future reports should identify it and distinguish internal tests from independent evaluation.

03 / Methods & transparency

Make the evidence inspectable.

These are the standards we intend to use for our research. Each published study should show what was tested, what happened, and what remains uncertain.

Define before testing

State the question, task sample, baseline, success criteria, and exclusions before interpreting results. Keep exploratory observations separate from planned tests.

Record the full workflow

Document model versions, prompts, tools, dates, and human intervention. Report time and cost where measured, including review and correction.

Report failures and limits

Publish sample sizes, unsuccessful trials, uncertainty, and changes to the method. Avoid general claims from a single prototype or a selected success.

Enable checking, protect people

Share reproducible materials where safe and permitted. Exclude private logs, credentials, and personal data. Clearly mark withheld materials and their effect on reproducibility.

Intellectual references & independence

Our approach draws on public work from Epoch AI on empirical AI research, METR on capability evaluation, Ink & Switch on experimental tools, AI Now on societal consequences, ARC on alignment research, and Inspect on evaluation tooling. These are methodological references, not affiliations, endorsements, or partnerships.

04 / About

Small by design. Accountable by principle.

A research practice for people working with AI in the real world.

Stream Presence is an early, founder-led applied AI think tank. Our focus is the practical capability, reliability, and opportunities of AI for individuals and small teams. This is a developing research program, not an established institution.

AI assists with research, analysis, writing, and building. The founder directs the work and remains responsible for what is published. AI-generated material requires verification; assistance is not independent review.

Future studies should disclose AI assistance, project ownership, funding or commissioning relationships, and relevant limitations alongside the work. Corrections and reproduction feedback are welcome through the contact links below.
