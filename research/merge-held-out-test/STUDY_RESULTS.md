## Predeclared verdicts
- **Success criterion: MET.** The evolution champion's held-out mean (3,553) beats RANDOM (1,014), GREEDY (3,390), and FROZEN INITIAL (1,639) as point estimates. Stated with its limit (corrected in Amendment B2): the margin over GREEDY was tested directly with the paired per-seed difference — +163.2, paired 95% CI [−321, +642] (bootstrap, 10,000 resamples, seed 42; normal-approx [−316, +642]), 55/100 seed wins — so the separation from GREEDY is not statistically significant at n=100, now established by the paired calculation rather than by overlapping marginal intervals; the separations from RANDOM and FROZEN INITIAL are significant on the same paired test.
- **Evolution vs its candidate-matched control: evolution wins clearly.** 3,553 vs 1,844; paired difference +1,708.72, paired bootstrap 95% CI [+1,362.1, +2,066.2] (saved script, Amendment B4), 84/100 seed wins. Directed evolution beat local random search around the starting recipe at the same candidate count (640 each) and the same fitness-episode count (7,680 each), decisively — but NOT at equal compute: evolution used 1,863,336 logged training steps against random search's 1,245,838 (~50% more; no total-step budget was enforced). This is a candidate-matched comparison, not an equal-compute one.
- **The humbling result, reported plainly:** the hand-written STRONG FIXED SOLVER scored 18,656 — roughly **5× the evolved champion**. Five minutes of naive weight evolution (640 candidates, 1.86M training steps) doubled the starting policy and beat its control, but it is nowhere near competent hand-written play. The skill curve is real; this improver is weak; the headroom above it is enormous.

## Learning curve (PROBE seeds 3000–3019, champions only, never used for selection)
- Evolution probe mean: 1,849 (gen 0, 32 candidates) → 3,692 (gen 19, 640 candidates). Noisy per checkpoint (CI ±350–850), monotone in trend, not in every step.
- Random-search probe mean: 1,849 → 2,001 over the same 640 candidates — essentially flat.
- Full per-checkpoint rows: `data/curve_evolution.jsonl`, `data/curve_random_search.jsonl`.

## Accounting
- Candidates evaluated: 640 evolution + 640 random search (1,280 total, failures included — every candidate's fitness is in `data/fitness_log_*.jsonl`).
- Fitness episodes: 7,680 per optimizer (640 candidates × 12 DEV seeds). No enforced total-step budget and no numerical episode-step cap.
- Training environment steps: evolution 1,863,336 moves; random search 1,245,838 moves (fewer because weaker policies end episodes sooner — a real accounting asymmetry of this game, noted in the Serpent card during debate). Candidate-matched is therefore NOT equal-compute (Amendment B4).
- Policy cost per decision: the policy scores each legal afterstate — up to four evaluations per decision — not literally one evaluation per move.
- Comparator construction: random search samples mutations around the FROZEN INITIAL recipe; evolution accumulates mutations and crosses selected parents. Same mutation operator, different candidate distributions — the comparator is local random search around INITIAL.
- Held-out records: `data/heldout_episodes.jsonl` holds per-seed scores (player, seed, score, max tile, moves) only. DESIGN called for action sequences; the runner discards them and the public records omit them, so the files are auditable score statistics, not full episode replay traces.
- Wall clock: evolution 291s, random search 226s (run in parallel), held-out evaluation under a minute. Spend: $0 beyond the Arbiter rulings (see below).

## What this proves and what it does not
Proves: MERGE has a true, measurable skill curve under the frozen protocol — a programmatic improver improved held-out play beyond its starting point and beyond a candidate-count-matched local random-search control (not an equal-compute control, Amendment B4), with the improvement visible on seeds used for nothing but measurement along the way; its margin over the greedy floor is a point-estimate win only (paired CI includes zero, Amendment B2). The strong solver's 18,656 shows substantial remaining headroom above this evolved policy (3.5k); it does not by itself establish that the game or this optimizer never saturates (correction per dot, Amendment B2).
Does not prove: anything about recursive self-improvement. Weight evolution is learning/optimization — the substrate. The weak part of tonight's stack is the IMPROVER, and that is the honest headline for the RSI program: the game can tell a better improver from a worse one, which is exactly the property a testbed must have.
