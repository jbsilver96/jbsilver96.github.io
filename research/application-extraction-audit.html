Experiment Desk · Application Extraction Audit · October 26, 2026

What the 372 are actually for, so far

On October 6, 2026, OpenAI published 372 claimed mathematical results at once — more frontier mathematics in one drop than the world usually produces in years. Three weeks later we graded the 30 headline families: extracted the tool inside each proof, asked whether it gives you something you can run or only proves something exists, and followed every citation forward. Scope, stated plainly: this audit grades 30 headline families of 372. The other ~342 were not graded in this pass.

The verdict
The motion is real — and so far it is all inside mathematics. Nine of the 30 families were built on by other people within 20 days; three crossed into explicit algorithms (all "galactic"); nothing reached a prototype. And across three weeks of that activity, not one family's application grade moved.

Grades (of 30): A — 1 · B-band — 6 · C — 10 · D — 13. The single A is a stopping rule: the Unique Games proof, if it holds, tells engineers the classic algorithm for a huge class of hard problems is provably the best possible. Negative knowledge is the only application usable today.

How to read this ledger

Tools travel. Theorems mostly don't.

A solved problem is not an application. Between the two there are five gates: 1 Verify (machine-checked proof) → 2 De-alienate (rewritten so humans can use it) → 3 Constructivize (existence turned into an explicit algorithm or object) → 4 Prototype (implemented and benchmarked) → 5 Deploy (shipped somewhere). Most results die at gate 2 or 3.

For each family we record the instrument (the new tool inside the proof, mined following the method of arXiv:2610.17090), the gate (highest stage with documented activity), the grade (A = usable now as a decision · B = method spillover plausible in 1–5 years · C = foundational, 5–20+ years, indirect · D = no identifiable application outside mathematics), and the chain — who has verifiably used the result since October 6. Every chain link in the underlying data carries an arXiv ID or URL we fetched and read. "No forward use found" means we looked — in arXiv, the repository's own history, and the web — and found nothing checkable.

Gate transitions

The moments the pipeline actually moved

  1. Oct 7 — family 109 gets a crowd. One day after the release, Doug Colkitt began publishing conditional improvements to the integer-multiplication exponent, built with Codex and assuming the unverified original. By Oct 26 the public repository lists a reviewed saving of κ = 0.0031 — roughly 2171 times the original 2-182 — while stating plainly it is not a verification and not a practical speedup.
  2. Oct 8 — family 158 is used as a stepping stone. James Davies extended the plane-coloring transfer theorem to dimensions four and up (arXiv:2610.12301); three days later a second team used what they call "the planar character-law rigidity theorem of OpenAI" to settle dimension three (arXiv:2610.18907). The first recorded uses of a release result to prove new theorems.
  3. Oct 8 — family 079 is load-bearing. The cone square-function estimate in four dimensions was derived from family 079's local-smoothing statements (arXiv:2610.12681) — an unformalized result trusted as an input within 48 hours.
  4. Oct 12 — the instrument mine opens. Ying Xie's survey (arXiv:2610.17090) extracted the new tools inside 38 of the 372 families — the de-alienation layer this audit's instrument column rests on.
  5. Oct 13–20 — family 003 gets its constants. Retellings of the Landau–Siegel argument extracted explicit constants (arXiv:2610.15483), applications to class groups and Chebotarev density were drafted (arXiv:2610.18491), and two papers nudged the 7/8 wall itself. The closest any family came to a grade move.
  6. Oct 20 — family 107 crosses gate 3. Rafael Pass published a self-contained constructive proof of ω ≤ 9/4 (arXiv:2610.28627): the flagship existence proof became an explicit algorithm — one whose constants are galactic. The gate moved; the grade didn't. That distinction is the whole game.
Methods

How this was run

The selection rule was frozen before extraction began: the 20 rows of our October 24 report's headline-claims table, 4 cluster heads from its applications addendum, and 6 famous named problems (Goldfeld, Falconer, Hilbert's sixteenth, Borsuk, Cannon, Hilbert–Smith) to fill the 30. Instruments follow arXiv:2610.17090 where it covers a family (20 of 30); where no checkable source names a tool, we record "unextracted" rather than invent one (8 of 30). Chains were traced through a sweep of all 105 arXiv papers mentioning the release (Oct 6–26), the repository's history.md, and targeted search; candidate links that could not be verified at a primary source were excluded, and the exclusions are recorded in the data. The three early movers (Pass, Davies, Colkitt) were re-verified at their primary sources, not taken on prior reporting. Grades are the October 24 addendum's unless new evidence moved them — disagreements are flagged CHANGED with the evidence named. This pass: 22 families carried prior grades, 0 changed; 8 were newly graded. Full records (every field, chain and source for all 30) are kept with the experiment's data files.

Honest caveats

What this ledger does not claim

  • The underlying claims are OpenAI's, and they are not confirmed. About 42% of the release's top-line results had Lean-formalized main results when the catalogue was last summarized; the catalogue's own field for whether a formal statement matches the intended problem reads "unchecked." Three manuscripts were withdrawn within a day and 14 revised. Every grade here is conditional on the result being true — several chains (notably family 109's) say so themselves, in so many words.
  • The audit covers ~30 of 372. The ungraded ~342 are most of the release's bulk and, by our earlier analysis, more D-heavy than this headline sample. Nothing here is a verdict on them.
  • Twenty days is nothing. Pure math's application base rate runs in decades. A D today is a statement about identifiability, not destiny — every D in the data names what would have to change for the grade to rise.
  • One reader, one pass. Instrument novelty assessments lean on arXiv:2610.17090's provisional ones. Corrections will be logged as amendments, never silent edits. Next re-grade: quarterly, or on the first gate-4 event, whichever comes first.
